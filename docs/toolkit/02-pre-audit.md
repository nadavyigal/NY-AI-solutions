# Step 2 — Pre-audit

Desk work. No client time. Two to three hours. The point is to arrive at
discovery with hypotheses to test rather than questions to ask.

## Inventory

| Subscription | Tier (verified) | Seats | Actually used by | Monthly cost | Capability the tier allows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then the number that opens the conversation: **what the business pays per month
for AI, and what fraction of it is used beyond drafting text.**

## Public-asset read

Everything the business already published tells you how it talks and what it
sells. Read the site, the social accounts, any published material. In the first
engagement this is what the voice knowledge asset was built from, without asking
the client for anything.

Produce: a one-page read of what they do, who they serve, and how they sound.

## Hypotheses to test in discovery

Three at most, each in this form:

> We believe **[role]** spends **[time]** on **[workflow]** every **[period]**,
> and that **[step]** is the expensive part. We will know in discovery by
> **[what to watch for]**.

## Risk pre-read

Before discovery, name what a wrong answer would cost here: client-identifying
data in a model, a professional-duty breach, an automated message that should
have had a human in front of it. These become the constraints in step 4, and
raising them before the client does is what earns the room.
