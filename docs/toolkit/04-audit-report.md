# Step 4 — Audit Report

Written after discovery, delivered before the roadmap. Ten pages at most.

## 1. What we found

The workflow as it actually runs, with the baseline from step 3. Show the
handoffs and the manual steps. The client should recognise their business and
see at least one thing they had not noticed.

## 2. Opportunities

| # | Opportunity | Value | Effort | Risk | Privacy path | Recommend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | H/M/L | H/M/L | H/M/L | where identifying data goes | now / later / no |

**No opportunity is listed without a privacy path.** State plainly what the model
sees, what it never sees, and where identifying data is re-attached. If the
answer is "the model would need the client's name", say so and score the risk
accordingly.

## 3. Leave this alone (required)

At least one process that works and should not be touched, with the reason.

An audit that finds only opportunities is a sales document. This section is what
makes the rest of the report credible to a cautious buyer, and it is what a
generic vendor will not write.

## 4. Risk findings

Four angles, every engagement:

| Angle | Question |
|---|---|
| Privacy | Where does client-identifying data go, and who can see it? |
| Error | What happens when the output is wrong, and who catches it? |
| Dependency | What breaks if the tool changes, prices up, or disappears? |
| Human control | Which decisions must stay with a person, and is that enforced by the design or only by habit? |

## 5. Recommended Quick Win

One workflow. Why this one. What will be built. What it will not do. What we
will measure, using the same units as the step-3 baseline.

## 6. What we are not proposing

Name what was considered and rejected, and why. This is where "buy vertical
software instead" or "this does not need AI at all" gets said when it is true.
