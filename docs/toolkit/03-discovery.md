# Step 3: Discovery

60 to 90 minutes with the people who actually do the work. Not the owner
describing what they believe happens.

## Rule

**Watch the workflow, do not discuss it.** Ask them to do it while you watch, or
to walk through the last real instance with the actual files open. The manual
steps that make automation valuable are exactly the steps nobody mentions in the
abstract.

## Trace one instance end to end

| # | Step | Who | Tool | Minutes | Manual? | What triggers it | What breaks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Follow the handoffs: every place work changes hands, waits, or gets re-typed.

## Adoption metric (agree now)

Pick the number that close-out will read, before anything is built. For example:
how many times a week the operator ran the process without being asked, and how
many of those runs needed no help. A handover sign-off is not adoption.

## Files-and-folders signal

Note it, do not offer anything. Does the candidate workflow live in folders of
files, repeat across many documents, or need to run on a schedule? These answers
are the first input to the readiness check in the roadmap.

## Data map

For each step: what client-identifying data is present, where it currently sits,
and who may see it. This is what makes the step-4 privacy path possible.

## Questions worth asking out loud

- What did you stop doing because there was no time?
- What do you check twice because you do not trust it?
- What part of this would you refuse to let a computer touch, and why?

The last one is the most useful question in the toolkit. It finds the human
review that must be preserved, and asking it is what makes a cautious buyer
believe the rest.
