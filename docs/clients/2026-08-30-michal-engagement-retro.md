# Engagement Retro: Michal AI Operations Pilot

Date: 2026-08-30. Client-safe: no client PII, no third-party identifying detail.

The first full run of the AI Audit Toolkit against a live professional-services
client. Ran 2026-06-28 to 2026-08-13. Setup is complete.

## Shape of the engagement

A solo coaching practice with a recurring billing workflow, a content workflow,
and no operating system holding either. Three tracks ran in parallel with the
operating-system track first, because the other two run on top of it.

| Track | What it was | State |
|---|---|---|
| C - AI operating system | Four assistants on the tool she already paid for, sharing one voice knowledge asset, each restating the privacy rule | Complete |
| A - Billing | Aliased session notes in, a sessions table and per-client draft messages out. Touches no invoicing system and sends nothing automatically. | Specified, workspace stood up |
| B - Content | Voice asset, content strategy build-out, publishing plan, analytics reporting | Delivered; analytics awaiting her real platform data |

## What this proved about the offer

**The existing-subscription wedge is real.** The whole operating system runs on
the entry paid tier she already had. Nothing was purchased. The engagement's
value came from configuration and process design, which is exactly the claim the
offer makes.

**Privacy-first is a design constraint, not a disclaimer.** The rule that no
identifying client data ever enters the model shaped every workflow: what the
billing assistant is allowed to connect to (nothing), where identities get
re-attached (offline, by the client, at the send step), and what each artifact
must restate. This is the part a generic vendor does not do, and it is the part
an insurance or legal buyer will ask about first.

**One shared knowledge asset beats several tuned ones.** Four assistants, one
voice document attached to each. A change is one edit. Four separately tuned
assistants would have drifted within a month.

**The last mile is the engagement.** Everything was written and paste-ready
weeks before anything was live, because standing it up needed the client's own
account and a scheduled session. In a paid engagement this is the difference
between delivered and not delivered. Book the live session at kickoff.

## What to change in the productised offer

1. **Intake must capture the tier and its limits, verified in the account.** A
   guess about what the tier allows is a design risk.
2. **Book the live configuration session at kickoff**, not after the artifacts
   are ready.
3. **Ambiguity about how the client's business is framed blocks the report.**
   One framing question was carried unresolved for weeks and it gates what the
   audit can conclude. Make it an explicit intake question with a required
   answer.
4. **A "leave it alone" finding belongs in every report.** This engagement
   produced one implicitly and it was never written down.
5. **No baseline was captured.** Hours, manual steps and handoffs before the
   change were never recorded, so no improvement can be stated as a number. This
   is the single biggest gap and it is now a required step-3 artifact.

## Honest limits of this as a reference

- It is a partner engagement, not an arm's-length paid client. It proves the
  method works, not that someone will pay for it.
- It is a solo practice, not a 10-100 person firm. Multi-person adoption,
  training and change management are untested.
- No measured outcome exists, because no baseline was taken.

Cite it as method evidence, never as ROI evidence.
